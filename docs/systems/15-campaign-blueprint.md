# Campaign & Year Blueprints

Files: `src/core/domain/campaign/` (`CampaignBlueprint.ts`, `YearBlueprint.ts`, `BlueprintSchemas.ts`, `CampaignBlueprintService.ts`, `BlueprintProgressService.ts`, `ArcModuleRegistry.ts`, `DeterministicFallback.ts`), `src/core/application/use-cases/` (`GenerateCampaignBlueprint.ts`, `GenerateYearBlueprint.ts`, `AdvanceYear.ts`), `src/ai/providers/BlueprintLLMProvider.ts`, `src/ai/narration/BlueprintPromptBuilder.ts`. Tests: `blueprint.test.ts`, `blueprint-llm-provider.test.ts`. The code cites `docs/partial-blueprint-yearly-plan.md`, which is not in the repository.

## What it does

Gives each playthrough a story skeleton, generated by the LLM under tight constraints. A one-time **campaign blueprint** sets themes, tone, hidden "truths" and long-lived NPC roles. A **year blueprint**, generated at the start of each school year, picks 1 to 2 story arc modules, assigns NPCs to roles, plants clue seeds in locations, and defines gates, milestones and climax options. A progress service ticks milestones off as play proceeds.

## How it works

1. **New game:** `GameEngine.initializeCampaignBlueprint` probes Ollama, then calls `GenerateCampaignBlueprint` and `GenerateYearBlueprint` (year 1).
2. **Generation:** the prompt builder asks for JSON only, listing allowed theme, tone, module, NPC and location ids. `BlueprintLLMProvider.generateJson` sends the schema as Ollama's `format` (built from the registry, so NPC, place and arc-module ids are enums and cannot be invented; see 18), parses and Zod-validates, retries once with a repair prompt (raw reply + error list), and returns `null` on failure.
3. **Semantic validation** (`CampaignBlueprintService`): module ids exist, at most 2 modules, NPC ids exist in the registry, clue locations exist, clue modules are among the chosen ones, no empty flag triggers.
4. **Fallback:** on any failure, `DeterministicFallback` builds a safe blueprint (discovery theme, lighthearted tone, no roles, the first arc module with one `yearStart` milestone).
5. **Arc modules** are two TypeScript constants (`institutional_scandal`, `forbidden_method_debate`), each with a three-stage reveal ladder, climax shapes and suggested roles.
6. **Progress:** after every turn, `BlueprintProgressService.checkMilestones` completes any milestone whose trigger is `yearStart` or a `questFlag` that is active, and emits `BlueprintMilestoneReached`.
7. **Advancing:** `ADVANCE_YEAR` (an intent from the resolver) runs `AdvanceYear`: it stores a summary of the finished year, generates the next year blueprint with a recap (completed flags, known NPCs, discovered locations, top subjects), and resets `yearProgress`.

## Strengths

- **A good guardrail design.** The LLM proposes, and the engine validates in layers (Zod structure, then cross-references against the registry), repairs once, then falls back to deterministic content. A bad response can never brick the game, and `wasFallback` is recorded.
- **Allowlists** keep the model from inventing modules, NPCs or locations.
- **Declarative content versus runtime progress** is cleanly separated (`YearBlueprint` vs `YearProgress`), mirroring the curriculum design.
- **Compact recap for continuity** across years, instead of sending full history.
- **Arc modules are authored, structured story shapes** (reveal ladder, climax shapes, roles), a sound way to get coherent stories from a small model.
- **Tested** (generation, repair, fallback, provider) and has typed events (`BlueprintGenerated`, `BlueprintMilestoneReached`, `YearAdvanced`).
- **Startup progress is reported** per stage in the loading screen.

## Weaknesses

1. **The blueprint has almost no effect on play.** From what I read, the generated blueprint is stored and saved, but nothing in the rules or the narrator uses it. The following are never consumed by any gameplay code: `clueSeeds` (no clue is ever placed or found), `gates`, `climaxOptions`, `moduleRoles`, `runTruths`, `longLivedRoles`, `toneProfile` and `runThemes` (the renderer is not told the tone), the arc modules' `revealLadder`, and `YearProgress.moduleStage` and `clueFoundIds` (never updated). The only live output is `BlueprintMilestoneReached`, an event with no subscriber. The player cannot tell the campaign exists. (Please confirm by playing a few turns and checking the save.)
2. **Milestones depend on the model choosing reachable flags.** The model's reply is now constrained to the flags the game can really set (derived from the content: lessons, places, spells), so a milestone can be completed. But nothing makes the model choose flags that fit the story or are reachable early in the year, so milestones may still be trivially easy or far away, and a `yearStart` milestone completes at once. The same applies to `gates` and `climaxOptions.preconditions`.
3. **Unreferenced content is still possible.** Semantic validation checks ids, flags and arc-module references, but not that milestone and clue ids are unique, that role names match the module's `suggestedRoles`, or that a climax is reachable.
4. **The seed is cosmetic.** The seed string is placed in the prompt only. LLM output is non-deterministic, so the same seed gives a different campaign, and there is no replay or sharing of a run.
5. **The campaign prompt cannot use NPCs.** It does not list the available NPC ids and says to leave `longLivedRoles` empty if unsure, so it is effectively always `{}`. The year prompt does get NPC and location ids, but `knownNpcIds` in the recap is always empty (see 14), so roles are placed on people the player has not met.
6. **Story content is a thin shell.** Two arc modules, hard-coded in TypeScript, and no authored scenes, events, dialogue hooks or consequences behind them. The reveal ladder is text that nothing displays. Nothing ever shows the player a clue, rumor, confrontation or climax.
7. **`AdvanceYear` is an ungated command.** The player can type "end the school year" at any moment (the resolver's `ADVANCE_YEAR` intent), skipping the climax, with no requirement that milestones or time have passed. The LLM resolver can also misclassify input as `ADVANCE_YEAR`. It does not advance the academic state (see 12), and the stored "headline" is `compatibilityNotes[0]`, which is advice to the generator, not a summary of what happened.
8. **LLM dependency at startup.** Starting a game requires Ollama to be reachable (the engine throws "Startup failed" otherwise). That is by design (the LLM is required; the deterministic blueprint is for tests and development), but generation adds up to four sequential LLM calls (two blueprints, each with a possible repair), so New Game is slow.
9. **Repair is a second LLM call,** a different mechanism from the resolver's. Generation itself is schema-constrained, and live runs rarely need repair, but a repaired result is not distinguished from a first-try one.
10. **Fallback is identical every time:** one module, one trivial milestone, so a failed generation yields an empty campaign that the player cannot distinguish from a working one.
11. **Hard-coded tuning and structure.** `MAX_MODULES_PER_YEAR`, the final year (7), theme and tone lists, and the arc catalog are constants spread across files; the Phase-1 comment says modules should become a content pack.
12. **Tight coupling to the quest-flag stringly-typed channel** (see 07), with no typed quest model, so story state is a list of free-text strings.

## Rework ideas

- **Decide the story's actual runtime:** make the blueprint drive visible, playable beats. A beat has a trigger (flag, location, time, relationship threshold), a scene (authored text or dialogue hooks plus a few choices), and effects. Clue seeds become real objects placed in locations or NPC conversations (see 13) with authored text.
- **Generate from a closed vocabulary of triggers.** Give the model a catalog of valid, engine-supported conditions (e.g. `attended_lesson(subject,n)`, `relationship(npc, trust>=x)`, `visited(location)`, `has_clue(id)`, `day>=n`) to compose from, so every milestone is achievable and verifiable. Add a validator that simulates reachability.
- **Make modules content,** not code: JSON packs with stages, beats, required cast roles (matched to NPCs by traits and schedule), required locations and authored fragments. The LLM then picks and parameterises modules rather than inventing structure, and the engine does the sequencing.
- **Cast roles from the real cast:** pass NPC summaries (role, traits) to both prompts and let the engine assign roles deterministically with the LLM only suggesting.
- **Wire tone and themes into narration** (see 05), and surface story state to the player through a journal: known clues, open threads, rumors.
- **Make year advance a scheduled event,** triggered by the calendar and gated by the climax, with an end-of-year summary built by the engine (what you did, grades, relationships) and used for the next recap. Remove `ADVANCE_YEAR` from free-text intents.
- **Make the seed meaningful:** derive choices (module picks, cast assignment, clue placement) from a seeded RNG with the LLM optional for flavor, so runs are reproducible and shareable.
- **Decouple startup from the LLM:** generate lazily or in the background, and start with the deterministic fallback if Ollama is unavailable (generate a richer blueprint later).
- **Use constrained output** (JSON schema) in the provider and unify the repair strategy with the resolver's.
- Validate pack-defined modules the same way as other content (see 09).

## Open questions

- What is the story for? Is it a mystery to investigate with clues the player must find and combine, a rivalry/relationship drama, or flavor around daily school life? The answer determines which beats, triggers and UI we need.
- How much should the LLM invent versus select? The current "LLM designs structure" costs reliability, while "LLM selects and fills authored templates" gives control.
- Is a seven-year campaign realistic? Perhaps one year, with a complete arc and a real climax, is the right target for the first playable version.
- Should hidden `runTruths` change what is true in the world (who the mentor is, what the threat is), with consequences the engine can check?
